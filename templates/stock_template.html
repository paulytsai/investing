Multiples in the valuation block use today's price and enterprise value for every year.
The forecast runs P&L → cash flow → balance sheet. Cash is the balancing item, interest is earned and paid on opening balances, and D&A is calculated from capex. The DCF follows Aswath Damodaran's FCFF approach: CAPM cost of equity with a mature-market premium plus country risk, a synthetic-rating cost of debt, a five-year fade to stable growth, and terminal reinvestment of g ÷ ROIC. Stock-based compensation is treated as an operating expense.
Scale slopes. A cost line's slope is the factor by which its cost per dollar of revenue is multiplied each time revenue doubles. It is estimated by regressing ln(cost ÷ revenue) on ln(revenue) over the fitted years; with fitted coefficient b, slope = 2^b. A 90% slope means each doubling of revenue cuts that line's cost per dollar of revenue by 10% (economies of scale); 100% means the line stays a constant share of revenue; above 100% means it grows faster than revenue. In the forecast, a line's cost at revenue R is cost(R₀) × (R ÷ R₀)^(1 + log₂ slope). Revenue stands in for physical volume, so price and mix changes blur the estimate: treat slopes as directional. Fits use the years after any break in the revenue trend (found with a two-segment log-linear fit), drop outlier years, and default to 100% when R² is below 0.3.
